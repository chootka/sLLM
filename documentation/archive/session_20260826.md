### 6. Method errors corrected this session

- **Raw band power ranked the wrong channel first.** ch0 has the highest raw
  1-2 min band power because it is the noisiest input (0.15-0.32 mV/sample
  against ch1's 0.02-0.04). Peak-above-local-background reverses the ranking
  and is the correct statistic.
- **Two band-amplitude estimators disagreed by 120x** before validation.
  Inject a known sinusoid and confirm recovery before trusting any estimator.
  Current one: 1.0 mV at 120 s -> 0.998; 0.5 at 1800 s -> 0.502; 4.0 at
  2100 s -> 4.000.
- **Phase-randomised surrogates are the wrong null** for a spectral peak; they
  preserve the periodogram exactly. Use coloured noise on the fitted
  background.
- `scripts/signal_check.py` searches PERIOD_MIN 30 s to PERIOD_MAX 240 s. That
  brackets this line, but its verdict needs a baseline file and was not used.

## Findings, plain language

A "band" is a range of rhythm speeds, the way bass and treble are ranges of
pitch. The millivolt figure is how big the voltage swing is at that speed.

1. The slime mould has reached two of the three recording islands. Those two
   electrodes show a voltage that rises and falls about every **2.2 minutes**,
   swinging up to **1.8 thousandths of a volt**. The third electrode, which the
   organism never reached, does not do this. Neither did any electrode before
   the organism arrived.
2. That rhythm is not coming from the equipment. The chamber's own rhythm is a
   different speed (4 minutes) and thousands of times smaller. The camera fires
   every 5 minutes and leaves no trace in the readings. Comparing the two
   colonised electrodes against each other, the rhythm is still there, so it is
   not one shared electrode moving everything at once.
3. The rhythm speed drifts between 1.8 and 2.6 minutes. Electronics keep near
   perfect time; living things do not. The drift is a point in favour of it
   being the organism.
4. The published work on this organism reports a fast rhythm of 1-2 minutes at
   2-3 thousandths of a volt. What is measured here is close to that.
5. The **half-hour rhythm the experiment was designed around is not there** --
   about a third of a thousandth of a volt where 4-5 was expected, roughly ten
   times too small. Only the fast rhythm is present.
6. The large swing from +28 down through zero to -6 thousandths of a volt is a
   separate thing and is not the organism's rhythm. It is the electrodes
   settling after the slime mould connected them, over about six hours per
   channel. It is not temperature: the chamber warmed up while the reading kept
   falling.
